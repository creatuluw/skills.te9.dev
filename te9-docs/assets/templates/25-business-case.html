Business Case

The business case for te9-docs

Who this is for: anyone wondering whether the founding day was worth it. On 2026-10-29 the founding session spent ≈13 hours building the document suite instead of writing markdown or commissioning an agency. The money question is settled — the hours are gone. What a business case can still do is hold that spend against the alternatives it beat, state the model that does the counting, and say what would reopen the case. This page is a model, not a measurement: every figure below is a labeled estimate, and the one number that already happened is the 13 hours.

Options considered

Three ways to get a document

Every document the suite produces could have been made three ways. Each option carries its own cost model, stated below the cards and derived in full in the fold. Option C — the build — is the path founding day took; it is the hero card because it is the one in service, not because the cards are kind to it.

Model assumptions · what each cost column counts
The whole model, in seven lines — estimates, labeled
  • Volume: 10 documents/year — a founding-year volume estimate.
  • Effort valuation: €60/h — an internal estimate, fully loaded.
  • Option A: typing is ≈45 min per document; the model charges A the full effort-equivalent of reaching the suite's presentable bar — house style, print, dark, consistency done by hand — at ≈7.5 h/doc. The 45 minutes buy a raw draft; the model bills the draft made shareable.
  • Option B: agency quote ≈€800 + ≈3 days turnaround per document — a market estimate, not a received quote. €800 ≈ 13.3 h-equiv at €60/h; internal handling across the turnaround (briefing, two review rounds, corrections) estimated at ≈35 h; total ≈48 h-equiv/doc.
  • Option C: ≈13 h founding-session build (spent 2026-10-29, sunk) + ≈20 min ≈ 0.33 h/doc thereafter.
  • Derived break-even: C passes A when 13 + 0.33n < 7.5n → n ≈ 1.8, during document 2. Against B: 13.33 < 48 at n = 1 — ahead from the first document.
  • Nothing else: no license, no hosting, no seats — the model has no other lines, and C's are all zeros.

Benefits beyond time

What the hours don't count

The model counts hours because hours convert to money. These returns do not fit the model — labeled unquantified, on purpose — but they land on the scale each time a document ships, and none of the other options delivers any of them.

One house style

Every document reads from the same Clay token set — consistency is the default state of the system, not a per-document effort.

Offline single-file

Each document is one HTML file with zero network requests — shareable by file path, readable on a plane, in a review, anywhere.

Zero recurring license

No seat, no subscription, no renewal. The only costs in the model are hours — and the big block of those is already spent.

Ownership of every pattern

Clean-room sibling: every adopted pattern was re-justified and is owned as a te9 signature. Nothing load-bearing is borrowed on someone else's terms.

Print + dark built-in

Paper folds force open and dark mode flips by token — part of the system, not per-document favors billed by the hour.

Sensitivity

Break the assumptions, the verdict holds

The model's softest input is volume, so bend it. The per-document break-even against A is a property of the rates, not the calendar — halving the year's documents moves the totals but not the crossing.

Risks

What the investment itself can cost

These are risks of holding the asset, not of building it — the build is done. Each names its mitigation; all three mitigations already exist in the suite's working protocol.

Risk · maintenance per templateEvery new type is a build plus a gate; left unattended, siblings drift and the corpus rots at the edges while looking finished.
Mitigated by · clusters + gatesBuild clusters share grammar so fixes land in neighbors the same session, and every template passes a browser review gate before it registers — this document cleared the same gate it describes.
Risk · staleness of brief contentEvery placeholder documents SUITE-BRIEF facts; as the suite changes, the facts drift out from under the templates and the corpus starts asserting a suite that no longer exists.
Mitigated by · the brief's own refresh ruleSUITE-BRIEF is updated whenever the suite itself changes, and affected template content is refreshed after it — the drift has a named counter-move, on file.

Mitigated by · stated assumptions + a recountThe assumptions sit at point of use, and the model gets re-run with counted hours at v1.0.0 — at which point the figures, not the estimates, carry the case.
Recommendation

The verdict the numbers allow

The case asked one question — whether the founding day beat the alternatives — and the model answers it at every volume the assumptions permit. The verdict is stated as strongly as estimates allow and no stronger.

Validated

The founding investment holds. Continue to v1.0.0.

Against plain markdown, the suite breaks even during the second document and saves ≈58.7 h in year one at the assumed volume; against the agency it was ahead from the first document and stays an order of magnitude ahead for as long as documents get made. The unquantified returns — house style, offline single-file sharing, zero recurring license, ownership, print and dark — all point the same way. The recommendation: validated; the queue continues to v1.0.0, with the model re-run on counted hours at that gate.

What would reopen this case

Three findings would reopen it, none currently true: counted build-plus-maintenance hours running at ≈2× the model at the v1.0.0 audit; volume collapsing to ≈1 document/year while ownership stops mattering; or a recurring license becoming unavoidable — the one line the model prices at zero. Absent these, the case stays closed and the remaining types keep building through the extension protocol.
