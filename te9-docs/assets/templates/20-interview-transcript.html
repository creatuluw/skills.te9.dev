Theme 01 · On starting clean

Why a clean-room sibling

The founding decision, opened up: why the suite builds beside its reference instead of on top of it.

Turn 01 You · asks

Q ·Why a clean-room sibling at all? html-docs was vendored, reference-grade, already working — why not evolve it into the suite instead of building beside it?

The Founding Session · answers

Because a signature pattern is only a signature if the suite owns it. Every pattern adopted from html-docs was re-justified before it entered the suite — owned as a te9 signature, not inherited. And the reference wasn't as consistent as its own book: html-docs states conventions its own templates don't follow — dark mode ships in 2 of its 22 templates, print support in 1 of 22. Here, the dark flip and the print spec are contract, on every template.

Turn 02 You · asks

Q ·So something got deleted to make the break clean?

The Founding Session · answers

Nothing. html-docs stays vendored and untouched — it is the reference skill, and clean-room means clean boundaries, not removal. Deprecation is decided, but at v1.0.0, not today. Until then the suite builds beside its reference, not over it.

Theme 02 · On the gates

One build, one browser gate

The review rule that shaped the build order — and what it caught that nothing else would have.

Turn 03 You · asks

Q ·One build, one browser gate — every template reviewed in the browser before the next one starts. Batch review would be faster. Why insist?

The Founding Session · answers

Because gate feedback on one template benefits its neighbors — that compounding is why the cluster order exists. Batch review was considered and rejected: the review of a finished artifact beats the approval of a proposal. A gate sees the document whole, the way its reader will.

Pull quote

"Gate feedback on one template benefits its neighbors — that compounding is why the cluster order exists."

The Founding Session · Theme 02

Turn 04 You · asks

Q ·And in practice — what did the gates actually catch?

The Founding Session · answers

The things a diff never shows: the h2 rhythm, measure caps, figure sizing. All of it via real browser review, never headless captures. A capture can prove pixels exist; only the browser shows whether the page reads.

Theme 03 · On what broke

Three figure bugs, all real

The figure pipeline broke three times. Each break left a rule behind.

Turn 05 You · asks

Q ·Figures — you said the pipeline broke three times. What actually broke?

The Founding Session · answers

Three real bugs. One: var() in inline style attributes — the indirection never resolved, and it painted edge-label chips black. Two: headless captures dropped the weight-600 mono labels — phantom missing labels that cost a dozen debug rounds. Three: flatten dropped alpha, and the video lost a quarter of its frames.

Pull quote

"The labels were never broken in the real browser — my preview loop was lying to me."

The Founding Session · Theme 03

Turn 06 You · asks

Q ·And the fixes?

The Founding Session · answers

Became contract, not patches: attrs-backed labels, CSS-owned theming, explicit dimensions, alpha preserved. Each bug graduated into a rule every template now inherits — the figure contract in the style appendix is that list.

Theme 04 · On what's next

Fifteen left, three clusters

What remains of the 35-type launch taxonomy, and what stays parked until v1.0.0.

Turn 07 You · asks

Q ·Where does the count stand? What's left of the 35-type launch taxonomy?

The Founding Session · answers

Twenty of 35 landed — this interview is number twenty. Fifteen remain, queued in clusters: formal & contractual (3), financial (3), formats & remaining (9). The plan is template 16's — the project plan's phase order holds, and nothing jumps the line.

Turn 08 You · asks

Q ·Last one — the step-player. The standup parked it. Does it stay parked?

The Founding Session · answers

It stays parked, as a nice-to-have. The core is proven — frame(t) timing and ffmpeg video export both work end to end; the proof sits in assets/experiments/. Revisit after v1.0.0. v1.0.0 first, experiments after.
