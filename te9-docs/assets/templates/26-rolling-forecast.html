Rolling Forecast

The v1.0.0 effort forecast

Who this is for: whoever picks up the build next, and the gate after that. This is the suite's rolling forecast for the v1.0.0 release: what work remains, what it is expected to cost, and — the part that makes it rolling — the stated rule by which the numbers re-baseline. The forecast is a living number with a refresh discipline, not a promise. Every forward figure is a labeled estimate from the effort model; the actuals are the one thing counted, real from the CHANGELOG.

Model · not measurement

Every forward number on this page is an estimate from the stated effort model — ≈1–1.5 h per gated template including its figure, self-review and browser gate round-trip, plus ≈2 h for the release audit. The past is not estimated: the 25 templates already landed are counted fact, from the CHANGELOG. Where the arithmetic shows, it is the model's arithmetic — nothing more precise than its assumptions.

The window

What the forecast covers, and when it expires

A rolling forecast states its window before its numbers: where it starts, what it covers, and when it re-baselines. This one starts at the last counted fact — suite v2.2.0, 25 of 35 types landed — and ends at the release gate. Everything inside the window is an estimate; the baseline is not.

Forecast window · baseline v2.2.0 · 2026-10-29

From 25 of 35 landed to v1.0.0 — three completions, one release.

The window covers the remaining work to the release: the formal & contractual completion — the cluster's three templates are landed (22–24), and what completes is its one open process, the RFI-2026-01 chain: responses due 2026-11-26, a published summary, and a possible RFP-2026-02 the answers will justify or dissolve; the financial cluster's last two types — this rolling forecast, then the cashflow statement; and the formats & remaining cluster's eight types. At the end of the window stands the v1.0.0 release step: the full-taxonomy audit, the README regen, the html-docs decision. The window re-baselines at each cluster completion — the re-roll rule, section 04. Until a re-roll fires, these numbers stand; after it, they are replaced, not defended.

The re-roll rule

How the forecast stays a living number

The forecast is rolling because a stated rule moves it — not a calendar, not a mood. The rule fires at every cluster completion and does exactly three things, in order. Everything else about the numbers waits for it.

Step 01 · Re-estimate

Actuals reprice the work

The completed cluster's actuals become the rate: hours per gated template, recalculated from what the cluster really took — figure renders, self-review and gate round-trips included — and applied to the clusters still in the window.

Step 02 · Re-stamp

The chips re-stamp

Every variance chip in the ledger restamps against the new baseline — ON PLAN, OVER or OPEN — so section 02 always reads against the latest roll, never the founding one. The first OVER chip, if one is ever earned, arrives here.

Step 03 · Slide

The window slides forward

The completed cluster leaves the window and its numbers harden into counted past; the window total re-derives from the new rate and the templates still open, and the forecast line redraws from the new junction.

When it fires · financial completes at 27/35 (the next re-roll) · formats & remaining completes at 35/35 (the last re-roll before release) · the RFI-2026-01 chain closing is a completion for the formal tail, not a rate change — its process effort sits outside the model.

What a re-roll never rewrites
The immovable parts — the rule's own guard rails
  • The counted past. Actuals were counted once, from the CHANGELOG; no re-roll re-estimates them. A worse-than-forecast cluster makes the forecast honest, not the history negotiable.
  • The denominator. The 35-type launch taxonomy is frozen until v1.0.0 — a re-roll moves hours, never scope. A thirty-sixth type is post-release extension-protocol work, outside this window by definition.
  • The bar. Gates inspect the document — plates, dark flip, print, folds — and never re-litigate Clay discipline. A re-roll changes the numbers; the quality bar is not one of them.
Assumptions

The effort model, in four lines

The whole model sits in the open — each line load-bearing, all of it labeled. If a future re-roll changes a line, the changed line and its date go in the ledger's reading note, so the forecast's history stays auditable.

Rate · per gated template
≈1–1.5 h — the figure render, the self-review and the browser gate round-trip included. The range is the honest one: inherited grammars land near the low end, new layouts near the high end.
Release step · v1.0.0

Window arithmetic
10 templates × ≈1–1.5 h = ≈10–15 h; + ≈2 h release → ≈12–17 h to v1.0.0 from the v2.2.0 baseline. The model's arithmetic, shown — no precision beyond it.
Outside the model
The RFI-2026-01 chain's process effort (responses, summary, the possible RFP-2026-02) is deliberately unpriced here — it is completion work for the formal tail, not template builds; a future RFP-2026-02 would forecast separately, in its own window.
Why the rate is a range — and which end is likelier
The range, decomposed — estimates, labeled
  • Lower end (≈1 h): a cluster that inherits its neighbor's hardened grammar. The financial pair does — this forecast and the cashflow statement reuse the business case's financial register, its plate grammar and its gate rhythm.
  • Upper end (≈1.5 h): a template that needs its own layout from scratch. Formats & remaining is eight new grammars — link/dependency analysis, before-and-after showcase, 2D quadrant overview, wikipage + index, compendium of archetypes, blogpost/article, journalistic investigation, tutorial — so the range's heavy end sits in that cluster, by far the window's largest block.
  • What dominates the rate: the gate round-trip, not the typing — which is why a figure-pipeline regression (the project plan's risk R-03) is the one event that would break the model rather than bend it.
  • What would move it: a re-roll, and only a re-roll — the founding day's actual rate was ≈0.5 h per template across 25 builds; the forward rate is deliberately higher because the remaining builds are new layouts, and honesty prices the difference.
