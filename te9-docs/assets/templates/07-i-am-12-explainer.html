The big idea

A helper that makes documents

te9-docs makes documents for people to read. Each one is a single file. Open it and share it anywhere.

It is a skill: a recipe book that a computer helper follows. The recipes say exactly how each document should look.

Every document wears the same warm look, called Clay. Warm paper, thin lines, and one friendly orange thread.

There are 36 kinds to pick from. The suite can even grow new kinds all by itself.

How it works

Four steps, start to finish

Making a document is a short story. Here it is, step by step.

Step 01 · the ask

You say what you need.

A school essay? A party plan? Say it in your own words. You also say what “done and good” should look like.

Grown-ups call this · Intake

Step 02 · the questions

The robot asks questions.

It wants to get things right. You answer, and it picks the best recipe for your job.

Still Intake · it wants it right

Step 03 · the copy

It copies a finished example.

Every kind of document has a finished example. The robot copies it, like copying a model essay. Then it swaps the pretend facts for your real ones.

Grown-ups call this · Instance

Step 04 · yours

You get a document.

First you look at it in the browser. It is like checking your work before you hand it in. Say yes, or say what to fix — the robot listens. Then you choose where it lives. Your folder, the big shared shelf, or anywhere you name.

Grown-ups call this · Review gate → Storage menu

Big words, simply

Three big words, made small

Grown-ups love big words. Here is what they really mean.

Big word 01

Template

Think of a finished example essay. Not an empty page — a whole, done essay with pretend facts in it. You copy it, then swap the pretend facts for real ones. That is a Template.

The real definition
From the suite glossary · word for word

A complete, finished, polished HTML document with realistic placeholder content — not a skeleton, not a component kit. The unit of the suite.

Big word 02

Review gate

You know that check before you hand your work in? The document gets the same check. A person looks at it and says yes. Or says what to fix. Nothing moves on until the check is done.

The real definition
From the suite glossary · word for word

The build-step approval rule: after each template is built, it is shown in the browser (or file path named) and the user approves or gives feedback; nothing is built forward until the gate passes. Feedback applies to the current template AND the conventions for all later waves.

Big word 03

Storage menu

The work is done. Where does it live? You pick where: your folder, the big shared shelf, or just a look. Name a spot yourself? Your spot always wins.

The real definition
From the suite glossary · word for word

The post-playback storage decision offered after a produced Document is reviewed in the browser. Exactly three standard options — (a) save to the project docs folder (<project>/docs/, <YYYY-MM-DD>-<slug>.html), (b) save to the central suite folder, (c) preview only, no disk write — plus option 4: specify location, which always wins when used.
