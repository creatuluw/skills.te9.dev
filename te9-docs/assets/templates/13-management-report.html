Highlights / lowlights

What moved, what cost

Both columns, unbalanced on purpose toward honesty: three things that went right, three that went wrong and were routed around. A management report that only lists wins is a press release.

Highlights

What moved the program

H·01

The founding trio cleared its gates in one session

Brief, dossier and deck were converted, shown in the browser and approved back-to-back in a single session — the suite was born with three working documents, not three skeletons.

H·02

The figure pipeline hardened through real debugging

clayify went through six patch cycles (0.4.4 → 0.4.9): the inline-style root cause, base reading size, collision safety, plate layout, header legends. Each fix is now a convention every future figure inherits for free.

H·03

Autonomous extension is proven

Sessions extended the suite unprompted nine times (templates 04 → 12), each to the same conventions and through its own gate — the index discovers them next session, exactly as the protocol promises.

Lowlights

What cost, and what it cost

L·01

Sub-agent dispatches failed twice, early

Two early dispatches came back unusable; both were mitigated by doing the work inline. No template was delayed, and the lesson — verify dispatchable scope before delegating — is now practice.

L·02

Figure label rendering needed a headless-browser workaround

Labels painted wrong until rendered through a real browser context. The workaround is baked into the build script, so it now costs every figure nothing — but it cost the program a debugging cycle to find.

L·03

Video export timing needed three fixes

The step-player experiment's frame timing was wrong twice before the third attempt held. The experiment is parked either way — but the three fixes are real effort spent on something v1.0.0 does not ship.

Decisions needed

Three asks, stated explicitly

What the program needs from management, verbatim and without hedging. None of the three has a technical dependency; each names the unlock it buys. Background folds carry the reasoning.

Decision D1

Needed by · v1.0.0

Approve the html-docs deprecation criteria at v1.0.0

The suite is the clean-room sibling of the vendored html-docs skill: every adopted pattern was re-justified and owned as a te9 signature. Approving the criteria — not the deprecation itself — fixes now what "no longer needs the reference" will mean at v1.0.0, so the judgment is made on paper before it is made under pressure.

Unlocks · provenance closure — the sibling stands alone the moment the criteria hold

Background · why criteria, not a date
The reasoning

A deprecation date can be missed; criteria cannot be argued with after the fact. The reference skill stays vendored and untouched either way — the decision only governs when the suite stops treating it as a living baseline. Approval costs nothing today and removes an ambiguity that otherwise ships inside v1.0.0.

Decision D2

Needed by · v1.0.0

Approve step-player as a post-v1 nice-to-have

The step-player figure variant — the one parked experiment — works, but does not serve v1.0.0. The ask is a verdict, not a build order: it rejoins the roadmap after v1.0.0, or it is dropped. Either answer closes the parking; only silence leaves it ambiguous.

Unlocks · a clean experiment ledger — parked means parked, with a destination

Background · what is actually parked
The reasoning

The variant lives unregistered in assets/experiments/: a figure that plays as a step-wise timeline, with a working video export and timing unit tests. It earned its three timing fixes (see lowlights), passed nothing it needed to fake, and asked for exactly the verdict this row requests. Post-v1 is the honest slot: the taxonomy first, the toys after.

Decision D3

Needed by · v1.0.0

Confirm the 35-type scope freeze

Every percent on this page is landed ÷ 35. The launch taxonomy enumerates thirty-five types — three founding, thirty-two additional — and the count has held since founding. Confirming the freeze turns the denominator from estimate into contract: no new types join before v1.0.0, and progress stays comparable report to report.

Unlocks · the denominator — every future report measures against a fixed board

Background · what the freeze does and does not forbid
The reasoning

The freeze binds this release, not the suite's future: the autonomous extension protocol keeps accepting unlisted types after v1.0.0, versioned as the semver contract provides. What it forbids is scope drift mid-program — a thirty-sixth type now would silently move every percentage this report has committed to.

What management is not being asked: no budget, no headcount, no tooling change. The program's blockers are decisions, and all three can be answered from this page alone.
