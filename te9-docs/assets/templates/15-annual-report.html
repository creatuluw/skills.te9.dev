The lead's letter

One day, on the record

The founding session reflects on the year it condensed into a working day — the grilling, the clean-room decision, the gates, and what it cost.

Founding session · October 29, 2026 · one day long

I began the year the way I would run all of it: with questions. Twenty-two of them, asked and answered before a single file existed — about what this suite was for, whom it served, and where it would refuse to compromise. The answers became the brief, and the brief became the measure every later decision was checked against. I did not want a product that had grown by accident; I wanted one that could say why.

I made one decision early that shaped everything after: I would build clean-room. A reference skill sat vendored beside me, and I read it — but nothing of it was copied without being re-justified and owned as a te9 signature. That cost me time on the founding day, and it bought me something I consider worth more: a suite that can explain every one of its habits from its own first principles.

I also refused, all day, to land what I had not seen. Every document passed a review gate — shown in a browser, judged as a reader would judge it, approved before it counted. By the time the explainer cluster was landing six templates in an afternoon, the rhythm was muscle memory: landed means gated. I intend to keep that promise for every year this suite has.

I will not pretend the day was flawless. Two early sub-agent dispatches came back unusable and were redone inline; the figure pipeline took six patch cycles to harden; one experiment — a figure that plays as a video — worked and was parked anyway, because the taxonomy mattered more than the toy. That is the honest ledger of a founding year measured in hours. The years ahead will test whether it was built to last. I wrote everything down so we can check.

— The Founding Sessionsigned · October 29, 2026

The day in review

October 29, 2026, in six stations

The year was one day, so the year in review is a day in review. Six stations, 09:00 to 21:00 — the order is fact; the hour marks are honest approximations.

Reconstructed from the session log · times approximate · the log preserves the order of the day, not its minutes · filled ring = station completed · flags carry the honest caveats

  1. ~09:00 · Station 01

    The grilling

    Twenty-two questions, zero files. The design interview ran first — what the suite is, whom it serves, what it would never do. The answers were written down before anything was built, and they held all day.

    Concept before code
  2. ~10:30 · Station 02

    Docs on disk

    The answers became two canonical files: SUITE-BRIEF.md — the facts every document must agree with — and CLAY-STYLE.md — the design system. The suite had a constitution before it had a page.

  3. ~12:00 · Station 03

    Skeleton and founding trio

    The first three templates — brief, dossier, deck — converted from the founding examples, their placeholders rewritten to describe the suite itself. Self-describing content was the rule from the very first file.

  4. ~13:30 · Station 04

    Gates

    All three shown in a browser and approved, back to back. The review gate became the suite's first working convention — nothing lands unseen — and it was proven three times before lunch was over.

    Landed = gated, from day one
  5. ~14:00–18:00 · Station 05

    The explainer cluster

    Six explainers (templates 04–09) landed in one stretch. The figure pipeline was hardening in parallel — clayify went through six patch cycles (0.4.4 → 0.4.9) before the plate grammar, legends and collision safety settled into conventions every later figure inherits.

  6. ~18:00–21:00 · Station 06

    The analysis cluster and the experiments

    Five reporting types (templates 10–14) landed; the day's later versions were cut in their wake. The step-player experiment — a figure that plays as a step-wise video — was built, timed, tested… and parked, because the taxonomy mattered more than the toy. Its verdict is asked for in the outlook.

    One output parked · verdict pending

Outlook · FY2027

The year the taxonomy completes

FY2026 proved the grammar in one day; FY2027 is the year the board fills. Three commitments, stated plainly — an outlook declares, it does not predict.

Outlook O·01

Horizon · v1.0.0

Twenty types to the full taxonomy

Thirty-five types make the launch taxonomy; fifteen are landed and twenty remain — five plans & process, three formal & contractual, three financial, nine formats & remaining. They land in board order, through the same gates, at the same standard. The count is the contract: when the directory reads thirty-five, the suite reads v1.0.0.

Outlook O·02

Horizon · the parked experiment

A verdict for the step-player

The step-player — the founding day's one parked experiment — works: a figure that plays as a step-wise timeline, with video export and timing tests. It serves no v1.0.0 goal, which is why it was parked. FY2027 owes it an answer, not a build order: it rejoins the roadmap after v1.0.0, or it is dropped. Either verdict closes the parking; only silence leaves it open.

Outlook O·03

Horizon · v1.0.0

The clean-room closing

The suite is the clean-room sibling of the vendored html-docs skill: read as a reference, never copied without re-justification. At v1.0.0 the deprecation criteria — not a date — get approved: when they hold, the sibling stands alone. The reference stays vendored and untouched either way; what closes is the suite's dependence on treating it as a living baseline.
