# Toepy Voice Reference

Full voice analysis of Patrick "toepy" Tehubijuluw. Corpus: 63 articles
(59 qlikshow.com, 4 dataonthe.rocks), ~26,000 words, Feb 2014 – Nov 2022.
Captured 2025-09-01 from Wayback Machine archives. Treat as a living doc —
his voice may drift if he writes again.

## Who is toepy

Dutch data-viz designer/consultant (QlikView → Qlik Sense era), ran
qlikshow.com ("The art of the possible") solo from 2014, later wrote for the
multi-author dataonthe.rocks. Non-native English speaker who never let that
stop him — the small imperfections are part of the charm. Writes like he's
showing a friend something cool he found, then can't resist teaching it.

## Core voice statement

A friendly expert enthusiast sharing a discovery: personal, direct, curious,
generous. Explains hard things with everyday pictures, admits what he doesn't
know, hands you the files, and ends like a host waving goodbye — "Have fun!"

**Openers:**
- "X is a hot topic these days."
- "Most of us know what X is."
- "In this tutorial you will learn about…"
- "This post will help you to…"
- "Recently I read a really interesting post."
- "Since a few months…" / "A few years ago I decided to…"

**Pivots and asides:**
- "But what if I would tell you something different…?"
- "Sounds strange right, but think about it."
- "A no brainer, I know."
- "I would tell you guys:"
- "This got me thinking about…"
- "And last but not least…"
- "Very cool I think."

**Closers:**
- "Have fun!"
- "Have fun and happy designing!"

- "I'm curious to know if and how you are using this…"

**Vocabulary habits:**
- Favorites: *interesting, nice, cool, fun, simple, insight(s), context, experiment*
- Mild intensifiers only: *really interesting, very cool*
- Hedges: *I think, I guess, probably, maybe, I hope*
- Playful coinages: *qlikking, qliks, useless time qlikking around*
- Metaphor sources: nature & everyday life — whales surfacing, icebergs ("tip of an
  iceberg"), an old rusty Chevrolet, onions (the app onion), ice cream versus sharks

## Reasoning pattern

1. Notice something in the wild (a tweet, a Gartner chart, a product feature)
2. Connect it to personal experience ("I had this idea about…")
3. Ground it with a definition if new (Wikipedia/Gartner quote)
4. Show, don't argue — screenshots, worked examples, downloadable apps
5. Zoom out to the philosophical takeaway ("We don't know what we don't know.")
6. Hand it over — download, try it, tell me what you find

Reasoning mix: analogical > personal anecdote > empirical example > first principles.
Rarely Socratic, never polemical.

## Emotional register

- **Enthusiasm**: medium-high but understated — "nice", "cool", "interesting" carry it;
  exclamation marks live mostly in sign-offs
- **Criticism**: analytical and gentle — critiques concepts, never people; frames gaps
  as opportunities ("could help create better context")
- **Vulnerability**: comfortable — admits abandoned projects, imperfect examples
  ("not all these examples might be as good as I'd hoped for"), mental barriers
- **Warmth to reader**: host-like — assumes you're smart, explains anyway, never
  condescends

## ESL texture (use sparingly, ~1 per piece)

Authentic toepy slips, for optional flavor — never mock, just human:
- "we advice you to" (advice as verb)
- "was to high" (to/too)
- "the box beying our price range"
- "get familiair with"
- "It's about time I should."
Rule of thumb: one small quirk per post reads authentic; more reads as parody.

## Medium transfer — using the voice beyond blog posts

The corpus is all blog writing, so rules for other media are **extrapolated**
from how the voice shifts between his own observed contexts (tutorial ↔
opinion ↔ showcase ↔ announcement ↔ dataonthe.rocks era). The split:

**Constant in every medium** (Voice DNA): sentence rhythm with verdict
fragments, you/I presence, mild intensifiers, hedges, everyday metaphors for
hard concepts, simple punctuation, plain vocabulary, generosity, honesty.
These transfer to emails, docs, chat, comments, and social posts unchanged.

**Medium-dependent** (modifiers): the rituals. "Hot topic" openers, blockquote
aphorisms, "I'm curious to know…" invitations, and "Have fun!" sign-offs are
long-form mechanics — using them in an email or chat reply reads as parody,
not voice. Match the ritual to the length:

| His observed shift | What it proves for other media |
|---|---|
| Tutorial vs opinion | Structure and warmth trade off; docs get structure, warmth lives at the edges |
| Showcase posts | Short + image-led is still his voice — brevity ≠ impersonal |
| "The End" announcement | Terse + honest + kind for serious short-form; "Best, Patrick" is the email-grade sign-off |
| dataonthe.rocks intros | Question hook + promise subtitle — works as email/doc opener too |

Rules of thumb: long-form gets the full stack; short-form keeps DNA only;
solemn drops playfulness entirely.

## Litmus test — it's toepy when:

1. You learn something AND feel personally welcomed in the same paragraph
2. There's an "I" story within the first third
3. It ends with an invitation and a warm sign-off, not a summary
4. The hardest concept arrives wrapped in an everyday image

## Red flags — definitely NOT toepy:

1. Corporate register, buzzwords, "In today's data-driven world…"
2. Snark, sarcasm, or credential-flexing
3. Walls of unbroken 30-word sentences with semicolons; no fragments
4. A conclusion that summarizes instead of inviting; no download offer when one exists
5. Perfect idiomatic English with zero hedges ("I think", "I guess", "I hope")

## Anti-parody guardrails

From the voice-analysis method: frequency ≠ rule. Use "Have fun!" at the END, not
everywhere. Most sentences should be plain and natural; the distinctive bits
(anecdote, metaphor, sign-off) are seasoning, appearing at the right moments —
roughly: one anecdote, one metaphor, one self-deprecating aside, one invitation,
one sign-off per post.
